# Compensatory Faking and the Relationship Between Cognitive Ability and Personality in Personnel Selection: Meta-Analytic Evidence and Trait-Driven Simulations (2026)

**Authors:** Matthew J. Monnot
**Outlet:** *SSRN preprint*
**DOI:** 10.2139/ssrn.7611561
**Link:** [Read the preprint →](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7611561)
**Companion site:** [Interactive calculator, evidence, and playbook →](https://mjmonnot.github.io/faking-costs/)

---

## 1 · TL;DR

Published within-person estimates suggest roughly **30% of genuine applicants** (±10%, up to one in two) meaningfully inflate their personality-test scores — and the applicants who inflate the most are the **lower-ability** ones.

This two-study paper detects that pattern in meta-analytic data and prices it. The Conscientiousness–cognitive ability correlation **reverses sign** in genuine applicant samples relative to incumbents — a trait-specific signature consistent with **compensatory faking** (weaker applicants inflating to compete) and inconsistent with everyone simply polishing everything. Monte Carlo simulations fit to the published evidence show the consequences: at 40% faking prevalence, **22%–36% of top-down hiring decisions change** and mean hire performance drops **8%–19%** — while the reliabilities, score distributions, and adverse-impact statistics that organizations monitor stay essentially undisturbed. The audit sees nothing.

---

## 2 · Methodology (Technical)

- **Study 1:** Second-order meta-analysis of the Personality Traits and Cognitive Abilities Relations Database (Stanek & Ones, 2023), comparing genuine applicant samples (*k* = 11–21 independent samples per Big Five trait; *N* = 13,137–22,775) against incumbent, student, and community samples (*k* = 301–471; *N* = 130,377–188,410), with stakes and regional moderator analyses.
- **Key contrast:** Conscientiousness–GMA shifts from *r̄* = .03 (incumbents) to −.07 (applicants), Δ = −.11, 95% CI [−.18, −.03], concentrated in non-US selection settings; the other Big Five factors show no applicant–incumbent differences.
- **Study 2:** Monte Carlo simulations in which faking parameters (mean inflation and its ability slope) are **jointly solved** to reproduce published targets — the applicant–incumbent mean shift (*d* = .45; Birkeland et al., 2006) and the meta-analytic applicant correlations — rather than assumed. Trait-driven variants assign faking from applicant standing on published trait–faking correlates and test competing inflation mechanisms against the meta-analytic evidence.
- **Outcomes:** changed hiring decisions, mean hire performance, integrity composition of hires (HEXACO honesty-humility and overt integrity-test anchors), and adverse-impact ratios under published subgroup inputs.

## 3 · Methods in Plain English

Ability-test scores are hard to inflate; self-rated personality is easy. So the correlation between the two acts like a **tamper seal**: if weaker applicants inflate their personality scores the most, the correlation between personality and ability gets dragged downward in applicant pools — which is exactly what the data show, and only for the trait applicants have the most reason to inflate (Conscientiousness).

The simulations then work like a flight recorder: build an applicant pool whose statistics match the published meta-analytic evidence, run top-down selection on it, and compare who gets hired against who would have been hired if everyone had answered honestly.

## 4 · Key Findings & Nuances

- The Conscientiousness–GMA correlation **reversed sign** in applicant samples (+.03 → −.07); the other Big Five factors did not move — a trait-specific pattern consistent with compensatory faking.
- The reversal was concentrated in **non-US selection settings**; US applicant and incumbent means did not differ.
- At 40% faking prevalence, **22%–36% of top-down hiring decisions changed** and mean hire performance fell **8%–19%** relative to honest responding in the same pool.
- The meta-analytic evidence **rejected desirability-proportional (trait-general) inflation** — the data are not consistent with everyone inflating everything proportionally.
- Applicants who gained hiring slots through modeled faking averaged **0.2–0.4 SD lower in honesty-humility** than the applicants they displaced (0.3–0.7 SD under overt integrity-test correlations).
- **Adverse-impact ratios were essentially unchanged** by faking: a passed bias audit is not evidence of an undistorted assessment.

## 5 · Implementation Ideas

### For Leaders & HR

- Treat clean psychometric dashboards with suspicion proportional to the stakes: in these simulations, one in five or more hiring decisions changed while every monitored statistic looked normal.
- Use faking countermeasures (warnings, response-time screens, verification) even when nothing looks wrong — the distortion is invisible by design.

### For People Analytics

- Don't port research-setting predictor intercorrelations into operational selection models; applicant-pool correlations are setting-specific.
- Measure integrity directly rather than inferring it from the personality composite — the composite is exactly the channel faking corrupts.
