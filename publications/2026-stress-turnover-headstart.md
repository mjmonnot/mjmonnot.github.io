# Stress and Turnover in Helping Professions: A Randomized Controlled Longitudinal Study of Head Start Employees (2026)

**Authors:** Matthew J. Monnot  
**Outlet:** *SSRN preprint*  
**DOI:** 10.2139/ssrn.7637538  
**Link:** [Read the preprint →](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7637538)  
**Companion site:** [Predictors, evidence, and what organizations can do →](https://mjmonnot.github.io/stress-turnover-headstart/)

---

## 1 · TL;DR

Head Start staff report high psychological strain. Drawing on conservation of resources theory, this study tests whether a short intake survey can identify, six months ahead, which staff will report elevated stress or low intention to remain.

It can. An 11-predictor intake equation beat the base rate for both outcomes on a held-out test partition (AUC .72 for elevated stress, .70 for low intention to remain), with a calibration slope of 0.92 for stress. Baseline stress and younger age carried the most weight for stress; lower job satisfaction carried the most for intention to remain.

---

## 2 · Methodology (Technical)

- **Data:** Randomized controlled longitudinal trial of 4,054 Head Start employees (95% women, mean age 43); median follow-up 184 days.
- **Partitions:** seeded person-level 60/20/20 split into training, development, and test (*n* = 2,429, 811, and 814); training for fitting, development for one comparison of four pre-declared candidates, test for one pre-specified evaluation.
- **Model:** 11-predictor logistic regression on baseline stress, burnout, anxiety, depressive symptoms, job satisfaction, hope, reappraisal beliefs, and demographics.
- **Evaluation:** log loss and Brier score versus the base rate, AUROC, AUPRC, and calibration, with bootstrap 95% CIs.

---

## 3 · Key Results

- **Elevated six-month stress:** log loss 0.532 vs. 0.596 (difference −0.064, 95% CI [−0.099, −0.029]); AUC .72 [.67, .77]; calibration slope 0.92 [0.70, 1.19].
- **Elevated stress, Brier score:** .177 vs. .203 (difference −.026 [−.041, −.010]); AUPRC .52 against an event rate of .28.
- **Low intention to remain:** log loss 0.386 vs. 0.417 (difference −0.030 [−0.060, −0.002]); AUC .70 [.63, .77]; AUPRC .26 against an event rate of .14.
- **Key predictors:** baseline stress and younger age for stress; lower job satisfaction for intention to remain.

---

## 4 · Practical Implications

After local validation, the equation could flag six months ahead which staff will likely report elevated stress or low intention to remain, while there is still time to offer support.
