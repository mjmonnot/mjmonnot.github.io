1. FAQ (methods, process, journalism stance)

  • - URL: https://informationisbeautiful.net/faq/
  • - Author: David McCandless (Reddit AMA answers hosted on IIB as FAQ)
  • - Read in full: Yes
  • - Principles:

- Start from incomplete understanding; research, assemble, verify until comprehension is rounded (data journalism).

- Naive questions help cover bases for broad audiences.

- ~80% data work / ~20% visual design; time split includes idea → research → clean → new questions → revise assumptions → shape story → fact-check → moodboard → sketch → design → polish.

- “You don’t design data when you visualise. You design your understanding of the data.”

- Transparency: share datasets; process/methodology as guard against misuse.

- Allow evidence to bend initial agendas (“gravity of the truth”).

- “Beauty” = aesthetic + clarity + data integrity + story + functional ease.

- Chartjunk critique depends on goal: engagement/creativity vs executive efficiency.

- Career advice: data science + journalism skills (story, questions, sense of what’s interesting).

2. Interesting, Easy, Beautiful, True?

  • - URL: https://informationisbeautiful.net/2009/interesting-easy-beautiful-true/
  • - Author: David McCandless
  • - Read in full: Yes
  • - Principles:

- Information needs to be interesting (meaningful/relevant) and have integrity (accuracy/consistency).

- Design needs form (beauty/structure) and function (works / easy to use).

- Two elements can be “tolerable”; missing the fourth of four tends to fail (e.g., beautiful but hard to use = useless; accurate and usable but dull = boring).

3. What Makes A Good Data Visualization?

  • - URL: https://informationisbeautiful.net/visualizations/what-makes-a-good-data-visualization
  • - Author: David McCandless
  • - Read in full: Yes
  • - Principles:

- Four necessary elements for a visualization that “works” (visualizes the Interesting/Easy/Beautiful/True lineage; includes story as a named element in later framing).

- Two elements → sketchy prototype (OK early); three without the fourth → strangely lacking (e.g., without story → boring; without function → useless).

- Backbone of his workshop process; presented as thinking aloud, not rigid dogma.

4. How I Learnt To Stop Worrying And Love The Bomb (Kinda)

  • - URL: https://informationisbeautiful.net/2009/how-i-learnt-to-stop-worrying-and-love-the-bomb/
  • - Author: David McCandless
  • - Read in full: Yes
  • - Principles:

- Absolute totals without context feel meaningless; reframe via relatability (destructive capacity).

- Initial clever framing can unravel when evidence contradicts the intended moral.

- Let the dataset change the mindset (here: nukes are not civilization-ending stockpile myth as imagined).

- Revise publicly when comments/data improve the diagram.

5. How Safe is the HPV vaccine?

  • - URL: https://informationisbeautiful.net/2009/how-safe-is-the-hpv-vaccine/
  • - Author: David McCandless
  • - Read in full: Yes
  • - Principles:

- Put alarming headlines in quantitative context; show sources and open data sheets.

- Update transparently when calculations or dosing assumptions are challenged.

- Present opposing-source claims (e.g., NVIC/VAERS) with caveats about voluntary reporting.

- Risk communication via comparative odds methods (National Safety Council framing noted).

6. Learn a concept-driven approach to data-visualisation (workshop note)

  • - URL: https://informationisbeautiful.net/2021/learn-a-concept-driven-approach-to-data-visualisation/
  • - Author: David McCandless / IIB
  • - Read in full: Yes (short promotional essay)
  • - Principles:

- Prefer an editorial/conceptual starting point over a pure data-first POV.

- Aimed at translating reports/research/messaging into graphics; bridging words↔design↔data.

7. TED Talk: The beauty of data visualization (official page + full English transcript)

  • - Official talk page: https://www.ted.com/talks/david_mccandless_the_beauty_of_data_visualization
  • - Transcript source read in full: Official TED English script as mirrored with bilingual presentation at https://digitalcast.jp/v/12191/ (English script section read end-to-end; TED.com transcript UI did not return body text via fetch).
  • - Read in full: Yes (English talk text)
  • - Principles stated in the talk:

- Information overload countered by visualizing patterns/connections and designing for sense, story, or focus (or at least “look cool”).

- Absolute reported billions are meaningless without relative visual context (Billion Dollar-o-Gram).

- Visualization as an “information map” / landscape to explore when lost in data.

- Hidden patterns emerge only when visualized (media panic timeline; Facebook break-ups).

- “Data is the new soil” (creative medium; viz as flowers)—prefer soil to oil metaphor.

- Visual bandwidth dominates senses; combine “language of the eye” with “language of the mind.”

- Absolute figures mislead; bring relative frames (e.g., military spend / GDP; troops / population)—“Let the dataset change your mindset” (Rosling).

- Visualization as knowledge compression; living images from curated, updateable data.

- Can visualize concepts/worldviews; design honesty forces holding conflicting perspectives.

- Information design = solving information problems (overload, trust, transparency, interest).
