How the digital poster was drawn

From Tufte to the modern bench

The printed board is sixteen tiles because a 4 × 8 ft board has to hang. The digital poster is one scrolling color page. The choices below follow texts we hold locally: author PDFs, extracted essays, and the reading notes in design_readings/.

Tufte first

Tufte’s 1992 Citation Classic note states the program as maximizing data density and the comparative quality of a display, and as putting a graphic in the sentence rather than in a box across the room. Powsner and Tufte (1997) put a 30-year clinical history on one page as a matrix of small graphs so recent values sit in a longer path. The PSA bars are the high-density object on this page. The 2 × 2 is a table wearing color. Uncertainty sits on the same line as the estimate.

Then the people who moved the medium

PersonWhat the local text saysWhat we did with the poster
Mike Bostock Bostock, Ogievetsky, and Heer (2011): “the document is the scenegraph.” Aesthetics go in CSS. Optional modules quote Tufte, “Don’t get it original, get it right.” The digital poster is an HTML document. Bars are CSS so they print. No D3 showpiece: hotel Wi-Fi and a PDF export still have to work.
David McCandless Absolute figures mislead without a relative frame. Interesting, easy, beautiful, and true: missing the fourth tends to fail. Those points are in the Information is Beautiful FAQ, the IEBT essay, and the TED transcript. Four pathway colors a non-statistician can hold: navy for agency, teal for communion. One idea per band.
Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic Audience first; a one-sentence Big Idea; declutter; takeaway titles, not “Findings.” Decide whether you are writing a document or a slide. Those rules are in her storytellingwithdata.com posts. Section titles state the finding (“the human ceiling is not one number”). Action sits at the end. No dashboard chrome.
Alberto Cairo Charts lie by cropping and by undefined “worst.” Readers finish causal stories the data do not support. Tufte’s rules are guidance within reason, not a checklist. Those points are in the 2019–2022 Functional Art posts. Confidence intervals stay on the bars. The communion interval is wide on purpose. We do not “clean” it.
Giorgia Lupi Data Humanism (Print, 2017): numbers stand in for people; complexity is not a pictogram problem; show that the data are imperfect. Seventeen people, not a heat map. Communion disagreement as a human fact, not only a reliability coefficient. No portraits of respondents.
Amanda Cox News graphics that feel like homework fail. Put an annotation layer on the graphic: show the pattern, leave some detail. Prefer “Huh? Wow!” over “Wow! Huh?” Those lines are in the 2012 Simply Statistics interview and the OpenVis / Eyeo notes. The page order is what we thought, what the registered tests did, what the codes then showed. Words sit next to the number. No scrollytelling animation.

Also used

Stephen Few’s Perceptual Edge papers (Common Pitfalls, Communicating Numbers, Visual Communication) sit next to Tufte on clutter. They did not change a layout already fixed by the Tufte and Knaflic texts.
